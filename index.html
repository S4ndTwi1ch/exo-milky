Physical Dimensions of the Galactic Disc

Quantifying our barred spiral galaxy across electromagnetic spectra. Formed roughly 13.6 billion years ago, the Milky Way's massive gravitational well is sustained by a baryonic disc embedded within an invisible dark matter halo.

DIM-01 // DIAMETER LIGHT-YEARS
0 ly

Spanning ~32.4 kiloparsecs from the outer edge of the Scutum-Centaurus arm to the far Perseus rim. A beam of starlight requires over one millennium just to cross 1% of this expanse.

POP-02 // STELLAR POPULATION BOUND STARS
0 Billion

Estimated between 100 and 400 billion stars. Red dwarfs (M-class) account for nearly 73% of this population, burning fuel for trillions of years.

CHRONO-03 // SYSTEM AGE GIGA-YEARS
0 Gyr

Dating from globular clusters such as NGC 6397. Formed barely 200–400 million years after the cosmic microwave background nucleosynthesis.

POS-04 // SOLAR GALACTOCENTRIC DISTANCE LIGHT-YEARS
0 ly

Situated in the inner rim of the Orion–Cygnus Arm (Orion Spur), roughly 8.18 kiloparsecs from the galactic core, completing one cosmic orbit every 230 million years.

GRAV-05 // SAGITTARIUS A* SOLAR MASSES (M☉)
0 ×10⁶ M☉

The supermassive central engine discovered by tracking stellar orbits of the S-stars (notably S2). Schwarzschild radius measures ~12.7 million km (~17 solar radii).

MASS-06 // TOTAL VIRIAL MASS BARYONIC + DARK MATTER
0 ×10¹² M☉

Approximately 85–90% of the Milky Way's gravitational pull arises from an extensive, spherical dark matter halo extending out to 650,000 light-years.
